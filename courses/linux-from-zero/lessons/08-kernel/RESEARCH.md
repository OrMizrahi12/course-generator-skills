# Lesson 8 research brief

- **Lesson:** 8 — See what the Linux kernel is
- **After:** After Linus / 1991 / engine picture: still do **not** treat “Linux” as the whole disk — the kernel is the engine in the picture
- **Audience:** Zero computer background; picture-first. Act II.

## Feature

On kernel.org, **Linux is the kernel**: a Unix clone written from scratch by Linus Torvalds in 1991. Beginners are told not to download the kernel tarball — they want a distribution. This lesson names that engine and proves *this* kernel’s name and release. It is not the whole disk.

It is **not** GNU (lesson 7), not GNU/Linux as a whole (lesson 9), not distros.

## Human job

A person just learned GNU is what they type. They will now call the whole computer “Linux.” They need the engine picture, then `uname -s` / `uname -r`, because those two lines are the kernel speaking its own name and version — not `/usr`, not XFCE.

If you skip the live kernel identity, the engine stays a metaphor. If you list the whole root again, you teach the disk, not the kernel.
